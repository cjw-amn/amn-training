AMN · Trouble ticket training

02 · Automatic opening

Ticket Analysis runs in the portal's background process at 08:00 and 14:00 (server time). Both runs look at YESTERDAY's data (the "end date"); some rules also check that a site is still down today. Only one run can happen at a time. Practical consequence: a site that went down this morning will not get an automatic ticket until it has been down for a whole calendar day, so it appears in tomorrow's 08:00 run. If something urgent cannot wait, it needs a manual ticket. The 08:00 run is the one to check each morning. Inside a run, closes happen first, then re-codes of existing tickets, then a refresh of each open ticket's subsystem status (SAT, BTS, CCTV, EMC, power, ARN), then new tickets. Automatic tickets send no email. If a run fails or is missed, the Portal Status page goes amber after 20 hours and red after 44 hours, and the portal team gets a Google Chat alert. Admins can trigger a run by hand.

AMN · Trouble ticket training

02 · Automatic opening

These six are the "managed" fault codes; the system fully owns tickets with these codes. Details. Site Down: across the whole site (grouped by the 7-character site prefix), the satellite (SAT) ping had zero successes and at least one failure for the whole of yesterday, and there has been no successful SAT ping today. BTS Down: the same test on the BTS ping. No Traffic: the highest daily location-update count is under 100 (2G). Low Erlangs: compares the last 7 days with the 28 days before and confirms over the last 2 days. It triggers on any of: a drop of more than 80 Erlangs a day; a drop of more than 25 a day together with at least a 20% fall in availability or call success; or recent traffic at 25% or less of its history. Days with 4 hours or more of downtime are left out of this comparison. Bad Availability: more than 4 hours (14,400 s) of downtime yesterday and on at least 5 of the last 7 days. Bad CSR: call success rate under 70% on at least 5 of the last 7 days that had call attempts, and yesterday must itself be one of them. A "Bad Blocking" rule exists in the code but is switched off. Note: the TT Stats page lists Low Erlangs in its LOW group, but the engine raises it as HIGH.

The engine works from hourly and daily aggregates, not from live alarms or the live ping page. Exclusions in detail: 3G (-Xu) and 4G (-Xl) cells are dropped from the criteria; cells whose live date equals yesterday are left out; stopped or decommissioned sites are left out; sites with "test site" set never get a ticket (they appear as "skipped" in the run output); a site that was down all of yesterday but is back today is not raised under any lower code either (recovered-from-down suppression); and the settings can exclude whole fault codes or site prefixes. AMN · Trouble ticket training

02 · Automatic opening

The engine keeps one managed ticket per site, keyed by the 7-character site prefix, across statuses 1 to 4. If the site already has one, it never opens a second; it re-codes the existing one instead ("modify"). A re-code rewrites the subject ("BTS: Fault Code"), the priority and the fault code, and adds a system comment "Fault code changed by Ticket Analysis". Escalations (to a more severe code) happen immediately. Downgrades: if the downgrade hysteresis setting is on, a downgrade such as Site Down to Bad Availability must pass the same three-clean-day test used for closing; until then it is held ("downgrade held") and keeps its code. If yesterday's KPI or monitor data is missing, a re-code is skipped rather than made on bad data. Why it matters to you: the ticket number stays stable while the fault evolves, so the ticket history tells the whole story of the site. Duplicate tickets for one site are a problem to fix (see Ticket Discrepancy).

AMN · Trouble ticket training

02 · Automatic opening

Everything not listed here goes straight to the country FE team at Open. Exact thresholds. Chronic site: the site already has 2 or more Closed automatic tickets created in the 90 days to yesterday, so the new one is at least its third; it goes to GNOC Triage at HIGH whatever the code, with a banner "3 or more auto-tickets in the last 90 days". Soft surge: an MNO with more than 3.5% of its live sites getting new tickets (at least 5), or a BSC with more than 12.5% (at least 5). The tickets ARE opened, but routed to GNOC Triage with a yellow "Soft Surge" banner. Flood protection: an MNO with more than 10% of its live sites getting new tickets (and at least 5), or a BSC with more than 50% of its live BTS affected (and at least 10). Those creates are suppressed (shown as "Surge" / flood_suppressed in the run history) and no ticket is opened. Flood protection only applies once there are at least 50 existing tickets. As GNOC, your Triage job is to decide: send the ticket to the country team (with an owner and an ETA), or keep it for remote analysis. A flood day needs a human outage response: find the shared cause (backhaul, BSC, hub) rather than waiting for site tickets.

AMN · Trouble ticket training

02 · Automatic opening

Both checks look at the NEW tickets a run is about to open, measured against live sites. Hard surge (flood protection) is checked first, at two levels. MNO level: the new tickets for one MNO are more than 10% of its live sites and there are at least 5. BSC level: the BTS on one BSC with new tickets are more than 50% of that BSC's live BTS and there are at least 10. When either fires, the creates for that MNO or BSC are suppressed (the run history counts them under "Surge", flood_suppressed in the logs) and no ticket is opened. Other MNOs and BSCs are unaffected, and tickets that already exist are still re-coded and closed as normal. The run also rates the flood's confidence: HIGH when the MNO ratio is more than double the threshold, or when 80% or more of the affected BTS sit on one BSC; otherwise MODERATE. Hard surge checks are skipped while there are fewer than 50 open tickets in total, and specific MNOs can be exempted in the settings. Soft surge applies below those levels: an MNO with more than 3.5% of its live sites getting new tickets (at least 5), or a BSC with more than 12.5% (at least 5). Those tickets ARE opened, but routed to GNOC at Triage with the banner "Soft Surge: Routed to GNOC Triage — elevated number of new tickets detected for this MNO/BSC. Please assess whether this is a network-level issue or individual site failures." What to do: for a soft surge, look at what the sites share (same BSC, hub, backhaul, region, power event). If it is one network fault, raise or work it as one problem; if they are separate faults, assign each ticket to the country team with an ETA. For a hard surge, treat it as an outage and investigate the shared cause straight away; nothing will appear in the country's ticket list. If the sites are still flagged on a later run that is not a surge, they get their own tickets then.

AMN · Trouble ticket training

03 · Automatic closing

When a site is no longer flagged by any of the six rules, its ticket becomes a close candidate. It closes only if yesterday and the two days before were all clean for every live cell on the ticket. Clean means downtime of 4,320 seconds (72 minutes) or less in the day, which is better than 95% availability, AND at least 150 location updates in the day (the location-update test is waived for 4G cells). A day with no KPI data at all counts as a fail. If the test fails, the ticket stays open and appears in the run output as "skipped" with the reason "closure criteria not met". In the example, the fix happens early on Monday, so Monday counts as a full clean day; if the site was still down for part of Monday, Monday would fail and closing slips a day. Exception: tickets on sites that have been stopped or decommissioned are closed straight away, with no clean-day test. Why three days: it stops tickets closing on a site that only flickers back for a day. This is also why chasing a country to "close the ticket" makes no sense: they need to fix the site, and the closing follows.

WHAT A SITE VISIT DOES

2 · LEAVE CODE

3 · RESOLVED (4)

Resolved, but still faulty?

AMN · Trouble ticket training

03 · Automatic closing

How the ACS write-back works: when an ACS dispatch carries a leave code and lists a ticket with "close status = yes", the ticket is set to Resolved (4). An automatic leave code resolves it directly; a GNOC-granted leave code resolves it unless GNOC chose "reject". With "close status = no", only a comment is added. The ticket is marked as resolved by ACS (resolution source: ACS) with the comment "Resolved by ACS site visit", and an automatic ticket keeps the fault code the engine gave it. Resolved is not Closed: Ticket Analysis closes the ticket only once the site passes the three-clean-day test. When it does, the Action Taken text written by the ACS visit is kept. A Resolved automatic ticket that stays faulty is one of the most useful things to spot: it means a visit claimed a fix that did not hold. The Ticket Discrepancy page lists these under "Resolved" with the reason the ticket should remain open.

AMN · Trouble ticket training

03 · Automatic closing

When Ticket Analysis closes a ticket it: sets status 5 and the end date to today; writes Action Taken = "Problem resolved — no longer flagged by ticket criteria" (or keeps the ACS visit's Action Taken text if a visit resolved it); adds the system comment "Ticket closed by: system (Ticket Analysis)"; clears the ticket link from the site's Monitor comment; removes the ticket from the FE Scheduler board (the history shows "✓ Done" only when an ACS visit resolved it, otherwise "Closed"); and triggers FE bonus processing for the visit that claimed the fix. Manual closes by a TT Admin do the same bonus processing. One known gap: closing a ticket through the full Edit form (setting Status to Closed) clears the Monitor link but does not remove it from the FE Scheduler straight away; the scheduler's own sweep removes it later. Prefer the "Close Ticket" button over editing the status.

AMN · Trouble ticket training

03 · Automatic closing

This is the lever behind steering the countries: field engineers are paid for fixes that hold, so the countries share your interest in tickets closing for the right reason. When a ticket closes (automatically or by hand), the portal finds the newest ACS visit that claimed it with close status "yes". For field engineers who were within location (1,000 m, within plus or minus 12 hours, from their tracked positions): an automatic leave code scores the entry as AutoLeaveCode (weight 1, or 0.5 for upgrade visits); a GNOC-granted leave code scores it as GnocLeaveCode (weight 0.5, or 0.25 for upgrades); otherwise it is Rejected. Drivers are judged on the leave code only. Month-end rules applied when the period is closed off: A, the ticket was still open more than 4 days after the visit; B, the ticket closed but the engineer was not within 1,000 m or had no location pings; C, a location rejection is reversed if fresher tracking data later proves presence; D, a later visit closed the ticket, so the earlier visit is rejected; P, a pre-installation visit earns no bonus. This is why a GNOC decision to grant or reject a leave code matters: it changes what the engineer is paid.

AMN · Trouble ticket training

03 · Automatic closing

All three are settings in the Ticket Analysis configuration and all three ship switched off; production may differ, so confirm the current values (the portal team can confirm them). Downgrade hysteresis: a downgrade such as Site Down to Bad Availability must pass the three-clean-day test; until then the ticket keeps its code and shows as "downgrade held". Escalations are never held. SAT-down hold: if a site's satellite link is down today (no successful SAT ping so far today and at least one failure), lower codes are not raised and the existing ticket is neither closed nor re-coded; it shows as "Held SAT" (deferred). The hold lasts at most two consecutive end dates, after which the site is raised under its own code ("deferral released"). Manual close: hand-raised tickets (not stamped as automatic) in statuses 1 to 4 and older than 30 days are closed on the same three-clean-day test with the text "Auto-closed by Ticket Analysis: manual ticket > 30 days old, site clean for 3 consecutive days"; some fault codes can be exempted. Unlike automatic closes, these send the closed-ticket email. A separate older "diagnostic auto-close" job exists (admin page /autoCloseTickets) but its schedule is disabled; treat that page as advisory.

Working a ticket by hand

What you can do on a ticket, what the system stops you doing, and why the ETA matters most

GROUND RULES

Steer the work; let the system keep the score

Do

  • Make sure every HIGH and MEDIUM ticket has an ETA
  • Comment on the ticket each time you chase
  • Reassign a ticket sitting with the wrong team
  • Get a manual ticket raised for faults the rules miss

Don’t

  • Change the fault code on an open automatic ticket (blocked)
  • Close a Resolved automatic ticket by hand (blocked)
  • Close a still-faulty ticket to tidy up: it reopens as a new one
  • Close by editing the Status field; use Close Ticket

AMN · Trouble ticket training

04 · Working a ticket

Two guard rails are enforced by the portal on automatic tickets: the fault code cannot be changed while the ticket is not Closed, and a Resolved automatic ticket cannot be closed by hand (the message says the system will close it once service recovers). Manual tickets have no such restrictions. Why not close a faulty automatic ticket by hand: the next morning's run finds the site still flagged and no ticket open, so it raises a brand new ticket, losing the history and the age of the original. That also makes the age statistics look better than reality. Why use the Close Ticket button rather than the Edit form: the button also removes the ticket from the FE Scheduler board straight away and runs FE bonus processing; the Edit path only clears the Monitor link. Comments are what show a ticket is being worked (see "Last Comment" two slides on), so chase in ticket comments rather than only by chat or email.

Open any ticket from the Trouble Tickets page (Operations, Tickets, Trouble Tickets, at /troubletickets) or by clicking a ticket number on the Monitor page. "TT Admin" and "Admin" are portal roles; ask which you have been given. Details. ETA: not offered while a ticket is in Triage. THE ETA

The FE’s ETA is the ticket’s due date

A ticket without an ETA has no plan. A ticket past its ETA has a broken promise. Those are the two things you chase.

The older GNOC and SLA due dates have not been set since September 2026. Ignore them if you see them on old tickets.

How an ETA is set and cleared

  • Put a ticket on a day in the FE Scheduler: the ETA becomes that day
  • Take it off the board: the ETA is cleared
  • Or Add ETA on the ticket itself
  • Board entries older than 7 days are cleared each night, ETA included

AMN · Trouble ticket training

04 · Working a ticket

The live due date on a ticket is the FE ETA (custom field "eta"). The AI Agent's "overdue" answers and the TT Stats ETA columns use it. The legacy GNOC_Due_Date and SLA_Due_Date fields have not been written since September 2026, but the ticket window still shows "GNOC overdue" or "SLA overdue" warnings on older tickets where those dates were set. There is no automatic time-based escalation in the portal: escalation is done by people, using ETA overdue, ticket age and last comment. The nightly tidy-up runs at 03:00: FE Scheduler entries more than 7 days in the past are pruned and their ETAs cleared, so unfinished tickets drop back into the "Open Tickets" tray and show up again as "no ETA". That is deliberate: a visit that was planned and did not fix the site needs a new plan. The stats pages count ETA coverage only for HIGH and MEDIUM tickets, so that is where the expectation is firmest.

AMN · Trouble ticket training

04 · Working a ticket

Last Updated (the top-level updated_at field) moves on every write to the ticket, including automatic system notes, Ticket Analysis comments and subsystem status refreshes. It is also the close time the statistics use. Last Comment (last_comment_at) moves only when a person adds a comment. It is tracked for tickets numbered 30000 and above; older tickets do not have it. Practical use: when a country team says a ticket is being worked, the last comment should show it. A HIGH ticket with an ETA that has passed and no human comment for several days is the strongest candidate for a chase. Agree with your manager how many days of silence should trigger a chase, and apply it consistently across countries so the conversation stays about the work, not about fairness.

Reference

More terms. Triage (1), Open (2), Pending (3), Resolved (4), Closed (5): the ticket statuses. Fault codes in severity order: Site Down, BTS Down, No Traffic, Low Erlangs, Bad Availability, Bad CSR. CSR: call success rate. Erlang: a unit of voice traffic (one call held for an hour). SAT: the site's satellite backhaul link. BTS: the base station radio at a site. BSC: the controller a group of BTS connect to. Subsystem status: the SAT, BTS, CCTV, EMC, power and ARN health lines shown on a ticket. Deferred: the run output's word for sites held by the satellite-down rule. Flood suppressed: the log word for a hard surge. GNOC: the network operations team that owns Triage. FE: field engineer. TT Admin: the portal role that can work tickets (comment, reassign, resolve, close, edit). Insecure Site and High Value Site: flags on ticket cards that affect how and how urgently a visit is planned.
